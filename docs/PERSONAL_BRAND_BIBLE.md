# Caleb Baldwin — Personal Brand Bible

## Positioning

**Working brand line:** PUT ME ON, CALEB.

Caleb is the Greensboro-based creator who brings viewers into real life, local finds, family milestones, and the work behind the camera. His audience should leave entertained, informed, connected to the community, or curious enough to try something themselves.

**Short positioning statement:** A personable, culture-aware creator showing people what is worth trying, building, watching, and remembering—from Greensboro and wherever life takes him.

**Brand promise:** Real personality. Good story. A clear reason to care.

**Audience:** People who enjoy everyday dad and family life, local food and businesses, sports culture, practical products, and the honest process of building a creative business. Core geography is Greensboro/Triad with room to travel and grow nationally.

## Content pillars

1. **Food and local finds:** restaurants, food trucks, drinks, local owners, quick rankings, “worth the stop?” discoveries. Give useful context and a real opinion.
2. **Build in public:** CB Vision shoots, editing, creator business, lessons, wins, setbacks, and the people behind the work.
3. **Baldwin life:** fatherhood, marriage, dogs, family routines, humor, and milestones, with privacy boundaries.
4. **Brands Caleb actually uses:** UGC, product demos, routines, and honest recommendations in Caleb's conversational style. Make paid partnerships clear.
5. **Greensboro and community:** sports, local people, events, schools, small businesses, and community stories.

Sports remains a recognizable storytelling lane across creator and CB Vision work. Keep a distinct client/program identity for each team.

## Voice and personality

- Conversational, funny, welcoming, confident, curious, and community-rooted.
- Sounds like Caleb talking to someone beside the camera: “Look…”, “so check this out…”, “put me on…”, “would you try this?” Use these lightly and only where natural.
- Can be polished, but must not sound like an ad agency, a news anchor, or an overhyped influencer.
- Use plain language, real specifics, and honest reactions. Keep natural dialect and phrasing when it improves authenticity; do not force slang.
- Captions should be short enough to read, with a clear opening and one CTA.

## Visual system — recommended personal creator starting point

Use this as a consistent creator system while preserving existing CB Vision and client marks. If Caleb supplies an official brand guide or logo package, treat it as authoritative and reconcile differences before replacing any colors or fonts.

| Role | Color | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Ink | `#121212` | Primary text, dark frames, clean contrast |
| White | `#FFFFFF` | Text on dark footage, breathing room |
| Creator blue | `#65C4FF` | Primary highlight, labels, underlines, key words |
| Warm orange | `#FFB44F` | Food, local discovery, warmth, occasional accent |
| Main Character pink | `#FF3EA5` | Occasional emphasis for family/beauty/fashion/CTA moments; use sparingly and keep the brand inclusive |
| Soft gray | `#D1D1D1` | Secondary labels, subtle backgrounds |

**Color ratio:** roughly 65% natural footage/neutral, 25% black/white, 10% accent. Choose one accent per video; do not combine all accents in every frame. Keep text contrast high and use color to organize information, not decorate every shot.

### Typography — proposed until official font files are supplied

- **Headline/display:** Anton, all caps for short hooks, series names, and key phrases.
- **Captions/body:** Montserrat, medium or semibold, sentence case.
- **Emphasis:** bold or blue highlight for one or two important words; avoid multiple font styles in a single frame.
- Keep captions large enough for mobile, two lines max when possible, and inside platform-safe zones. Leave faces, food, hands, and product labels visible.
- For client content, use the client's approved fonts instead.

## Camera and image direction

- Warm, natural, documentary feel. Let real people, food, motion, and reactions lead.
- Prioritize close details, hands at work, establishing context, movement, and authentic reaction shots.
- Mix clean professional camera footage with phone footage when it serves the story; do not fake a candid moment.
- Keep skin tones believable, food colors appetizing, and highlights controlled. Avoid heavy orange/teal grades, over-sharpening, fake film burns, and template clutter.
- Graphics organize the story; they should never hide strong footage.

## Repeatable series and format ideas

- **PUT ME ON:** a place, product, person, or experience Caleb thinks viewers should know.
- **Worth the Stop?** quick local review with the real verdict.
- **Baldwin Crew:** family moments and new-dad learning curves.
- **Behind the Vision:** shoots, client work, and the making of a project.
- **Triad Roll Call:** local people, food, sports, and community finds.

Treat these as reusable labels, not requirements. Do not force a series name onto unrelated footage.

## Short-form storytelling framework

Default target: 20–45 seconds, adjusted to the story and platform.

1. **Hook (0–2s):** clear statement, question, visual surprise, or strongest reaction.
2. **Proof (2–8s):** show the place/product/person or context immediately.
3. **Story (8–28s):** the meaningful moment, useful details, or personality.
4. **Verdict/payoff (final 5–10s):** Caleb's actual take or the story's result.
5. **Conversation:** one natural CTA, e.g. “Would you try it?” or “What should I check out next?”

Adapt structure to the footage. Do not manufacture a verdict or reaction.

## Caption style

- Lead with an idea or detail that earns attention, not “Hey guys.”
- Use natural language and specific local/product names.
- Keep one primary call to action.
- Add location and relevant tags when useful; avoid stuffing hashtags.
- For a partnership, clearly label sponsorship/paid relationship and only claim what Caleb can support.

## Brand boundaries

- No guaranteed results, fake controversy, invented quotes, or exaggerated product claims.
- Do not show the baby's private information, medical details, home address, school/daycare identifiers, license plates, or private family moments without Caleb's explicit direction.
- Respect client confidentiality, release forms, school/student permissions, and music usage rights.
- Avoid flattening every post into sales content. Let personality and community stories build trust.
